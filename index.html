About

I am a physicist working on halide perovskite solar cells. My research asks a practical question: why do these devices degrade under real operating conditions, and which molecular and interfacial design choices actually make them last?

I completed my DPhil in Prof. Henry Snaith's group at Oxford, combining device fabrication with operando synchrotron measurements at Diamond Light Source and the ESRF. I was scientific lead on three of nine beamtime campaigns, and I build Python workflows to process, analyse and visualise the optical, structural and device data that come out of them.

My work also covers electrocatalysts for hydrogen production — the subject of my MEng at Tsinghua — and, through the Innovate UK ICURe programme, how research insights can be translated into practical impact.

Selected research

R1

Ion-driven degradation in perovskite solar cells

A molecular hole-conductor blend that suppresses ion migration and extends operational stability, supported by operando structural evidence linking molecular design to device reliability.

Stability · Hole transport · Operando

R2

Watching films evolve at synchrotron beamlines

In-situ and operando GIWAXS at Diamond Light Source and the ESRF, resolving the crystallisation, annealing and degradation pathways of solution-processed and vapour-deposited perovskite films as they happen.

GIWAXS · Synchrotron · In situ

R3

Interfaces, passivation and fabrication

How crystal facets, surface chemistry and deposition route govern passivation effectiveness and long-term device reliability — from bandgap-universal passivation to all-vacuum-deposited absorbers.

Interfaces · Passivation · Fabrication
